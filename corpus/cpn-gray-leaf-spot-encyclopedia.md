# Gray Leaf Spot of Corn

## Overview

Gray leaf spot is caused by the fungus *Cercospora zeae-maydis*. It occurs virtually every
growing season and can cause economic losses when environmental conditions support its
development.

## Symptoms and identification

Initial symptoms appear on the lower leaves roughly two to three weeks before tasseling.
Characteristic lesions are:

- Long (up to 2 inches) and narrow
- Rectangular in shape
- Light tan at first, later turning gray
- Typically bounded by the leaf veins
- Able to coalesce, ultimately destroying entire leaves

## Disease cycle

The fungus survives in corn residue, which makes the disease more prevalent in continuous
corn. Spores are dispersed by wind and water splash, infect leaves, and the disease progresses
when environmental conditions are favorable.

## Conditions that favor disease

Disease development requires warm (80s °F) and humid weather — relative humidity above 90
percent for 12 or more hours. Overall severity depends on hybrid susceptibility and on the
environment.

## Management

**Cultural practices**

- Select resistant hybrids and inbreds.
- Crop rotation and tillage reduce survival of the fungus in residue.

**Chemical control**

Foliar fungicides labeled for gray leaf spot are available for in-season management.
