# Southern Rust of Corn

Summarized from the Crop Protection Network encyclopedia entry. See the URL above for
the original text, photographs, and the disease cycle diagram.

## Pathogen

Southern rust is caused by the fungus *Puccinia polysora*. It is a different pathogen from
common rust (*Puccinia sorghi*), and unlike common rust it can cause economically significant
yield loss in field corn.

## Symptoms and signs

Pustules appear primarily on the upper leaf surface and on leaf sheaths. They are usually
circular or oval, very numerous, and densely scattered. Pustules emerge orange and then
darken to chocolate brown or black, often forming dark circles around the original pustule.

## Telling southern rust from common rust

Three observations used together — pustule color, pustule density, and which side of the leaf
the pustules are on — are what separate the two in the field. Color alone is not reliable.

| | Common rust (*P. sorghi*) | Southern rust (*P. polysora*) |
|---|---|---|
| Pustule color | Brick-red to reddish brown | Orange, darkening to chocolate brown or black |
| Pustule shape | Oval to elongated | Circular to oval, smaller |
| Density | Sparsely scattered | Very numerous, densely clustered |
| Leaf surface | Both upper and lower surfaces | Almost exclusively the upper surface |

## Conditions that favor disease

Southern rust is favored by high humidity and temperatures around 80 °F. It is warmer-weather
disease than common rust, which prefers night temperatures of 65-70 °F. The pathogen does not
overwinter in the Corn Belt; spores blow north each season, so arrival timing varies year to
year. The Corn ipmPIPE tracking tool is used to follow its seasonal movement north.

## Management

- Resistant hybrids and inbreds are available.
- Foliar fungicides labeled for southern rust are available. Because arrival timing varies,
  the decision depends on when the disease shows up relative to growth stage.
