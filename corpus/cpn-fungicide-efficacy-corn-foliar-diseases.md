# Fungicide Efficacy for Control of Corn Foliar Diseases

Publication CPN-2011-W. DOI: doi.org/10.31274/cpn-20190620-002. Table version 02/2025.

## How the ratings were determined

The Corn Disease Working Group (CDWG) developed ratings for how well fungicides control major
corn diseases in the United States. The CDWG determined efficacy ratings for each fungicide
listed in the table by field testing the materials over multiple years and locations. Ratings
are based on the product's level of disease control and do not necessarily reflect yield
increases obtained from product application.

A product's efficacy depends upon proper application timing, rate, and application method as
determined by the product label, and on the overall disease level in the field at the time of
application. Differences in efficacy among products were determined by directly comparing
products in field tests using a single application of the labeled rate. For application timing
and use considerations, contact your local cooperative extension service.

The table is not intended to be a list of all labeled products. Additional fungicides are
labeled for disease on corn. Other fungicides may be available for diseases not listed in the
table, including Diplodia, Gibberella, and Fusarium ear rots. Many products have specific use
restrictions about the amount of active ingredient that can be applied within a period of time
or the number of sequential applications. Read and follow all use restrictions prior to
applying any fungicide.

## Rating scale

- **P** = Poor
- **F** = Fair
- **G** = Good
- **VG** = Very Good
- **E** = Excellent
- **NL** = Not labeled for use against this disease
- **U** = Labeled, but unknown efficacy or insufficient data to rank

## Fungicide mode-of-action (FRAC) groups used in the table

- **Group 11** — QoI strobilurins
- **Group 3** — DMI triazoles
- **Group 7** — SDHI

Rotating and mixing modes of action is the basis of fungicide resistance management; products
combining more than one FRAC group are marked in the table as mixed-class products.

## Footnotes and caveats from the publication

1. Fungicide application timing is extremely important and needs to be made near the onset of
   tar spot symptoms (applies to the tar spot column of the original table).
2. Harvest restrictions are listed for field corn harvested for grain. Restrictions may vary
   for other types of corn (sweet, seed, popcorn, etc.) and corn for other uses such as forage
   or fodder.
3. A 2(ee) label is available for several fungicides for control of tar spot, however efficacy
   data are limited. Check 2(ee) labels carefully, as not all products have 2(ee) labels in all
   states.
