# Common Rust — Nebraska Extension CropWatch

## Pathogen

The causal agent is *Puccinia sorghi* Schwein, distributed widely throughout the U.S. Corn Belt.
The fungus overwinters in tropical and subtropical locations where it completes its complex life
cycle (five different spore stages) on alternate hosts (*Oxalis* species).

In Nebraska, disease development depends on wind transport of urediniospores from southern
regions such as Mexico and Texas during early to mid-June. Multiple races of the pathogen exist.

## Disease symptoms

Initial lesions appear as small, circular to elongate spots, frequently clustered together. As
the disease progresses, pustules rupture through the leaf surface and develop a prominent yellow
halo. Brownish-red oblong pustules are the characteristic symptom on leaves; the urediniospores
that rub off on fingers are what impart the color to the lesion.

Unlike southern rust, common rust lesions are distributed sparsely across both upper and lower
leaf surfaces. Spores are wind-blown, with new infections occurring every 7 to 14 days. As the
season advances, black teliospores develop within lesions and eventually become the dominant
spore type.

## Favorable environmental conditions

Cool temperatures between 60-80 °F combined with high humidity favor disease development.

## Management

**Genetic resistance.** Resistant varieties are the most cost-effective means to manage common
rust. Consult your seed dealer to determine the best hybrids and inbreds for your area.

**Cultural practices.** Because the pathogen cannot survive Nebraska winters, cultivation and
crop rotation do not affect common rust development.
