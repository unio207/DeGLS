# Fungicide decisions in 2026

As corn enters the tassel stage, fungicide application decisions become urgent. Research
demonstrates that the best time to apply a fungicide to manage foliar diseases is at tassel (VT)
through milk (R3) stage, yet growers should carefully evaluate whether spraying is economically
justified given tight margins.

## Disease risk assessment during grain fill

### Tar spot

As of June 30, 2026, tar spot had been documented in 10 Iowa counties. Tar spot is favored by
extended periods (30 days) of mild temperatures (64-73 °F). During July and August, warmer
conditions significantly inhibit disease progression, though risk resurges in September as
temperatures moderate.

### Southern rust

Southern rust activity remained geographically limited in 2026. Only four Texas counties
reported infections by June 30, 2026, compared with several counties in Alabama, Arkansas,
Florida, Georgia, Mississippi and Texas by June 29, 2025. This substantially reduced southern
inoculum source means Iowa's southern rust risk remained low.

## Return on investment considerations

Disease severity directly correlates with fungicide profitability. Research from Purdue
University's Dr. Darcy Telenko found that when tar spot severity was high (>5% at dent, R5), a
positive ROI on a fungicide application occurred (average of $110 for several products applied at
VT through R3). When severity remained below 5%, partial returns were negative (average of -$20
for several products applied at VT through R3).

The critical challenge is predicting whether disease severity at R5 will exceed the 5%
profitability threshold at the time of the VT application. The CPN Crop Risk Tool and the weather
outlook are useful: because foliar diseases require moisture to develop, dry conditions during
grain fill suggest low disease risk and potentially poor fungicide ROI. A Fungicide ROI
Calculator is available through the Crop Protection Network.

## Fungicide product efficacy

Product selection matters. Fungicides differ in effectiveness against specific diseases. The Crop
Protection Network publishes both a fungicide efficacy table and an interactive efficacy tool.

## Hybrid susceptibility

Robertson Lab research conducted with Wyffels in 2025 evaluated five fungicide application
timings across two hybrids at three Iowa locations, comparing disease-susceptible and
disease-tolerant varieties under severe disease conditions. At Muscatine Island Research and
Demonstration Farm, tar spot severity on non-sprayed control plots reached 17% and 8% at R5 for
susceptible and tolerant hybrids respectively. At the Northeast location, southern rust severity
hit 39% and 18% at R5 for susceptible and tolerant hybrids.

Yield responses and economic returns substantially favored the susceptible hybrid.
Disease-tolerant hybrids showed minimal fungicide benefit, suggesting a fungicide application is
not necessary on defensive hybrids.

## Planting date effects

Planting date significantly influences foliar disease pressure and fungicide necessity.
Three-location trials in 2025 demonstrated that southern rust was more severe on corn planted in
mid-late May compared with corn planted in mid-late April. Southern rust severity on
late-planted corn receiving fungicide at R1 exceeded that of early-planted corn receiving no
fungicide at two locations.

### Southern rust severity at R5 by planting date and fungicide application (2025)

| Planting date | Fungicide applied | SERF (%) | AEA (%) | NWRF (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early April | No | 8.30 | 1.00 | 41.00 |
| Early April | Yes | 1.57 | 0.55 | 19.80 |
| Late May | No | 45.00 | 15.52 | 64.50 |
| Late May | Yes | 16.77 | 13.53 | 18.00 |

Early-season plantings develop less severe disease, potentially eliminating fungicide
requirements. Dr. Pierce Paul's Ohio State research similarly documented that late-planted corn
had greater gray leaf spot severity than early-planted corn.
