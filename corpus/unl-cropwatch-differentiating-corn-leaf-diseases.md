# Differentiating Corn Leaf Diseases

## Risk factors

Disease risk increases with these conditions:

- Susceptible hybrid
- Continuous corn
- Prior severe disease history in the field
- Minimum or no-till systems
- Recent or forecasted wet weather or high humidity

## Bacterial leaf streak

Yellow to tan streaks develop between the veins, typically beginning on the lower leaves. These
lesions have wavy margins and irregular shapes and show a yellow hue when backlit. They are
often confused with gray leaf spot, but the distinguishing feature is their irregular or wavy
edges rather than the straight, smooth margins characteristic of fungal lesions.

## Gray leaf spot

Gray to tan lesions develop between the veins and are distinctly rectangular with smooth, linear
margins. Development requires 14-21 days and begins in the lower leaves. These fungal lesions
appear less yellow than bacterial leaf streak lesions and develop during wet, humid, and warm
conditions.

## Goss's bacterial wilt and blight

Lesions can be large and water-soaked with very irregular shapes. The defining diagnostic
features are dark spots or "freckles" at the lesion edges, plus shiny bacterial exudate on
either the upper or lower leaf surface. The bacterium exploits wounds from hail or wind.

## Northern corn leaf blight

This fungal disease produces lesions that are usually tan in color and cigar-shaped with rounded
ends. They vary from small to large and have smooth, rounded margins. Under humid conditions,
mature lesions may appear dusty as fungal spores develop.

## Physoderma brown spot

Caused by a fungus-like organism that requires water for infection via swimming spores. Lesions
on leaf blades are smaller and tan to yellow, while lesions in the midrib are larger and darker
in color. Characteristically, lesions appear in bands across leaves during alternating wet-dry
cycles, particularly when infection occurs in the whorl as leaves emerge. Nodal infections can
weaken stalks, increasing lodging vulnerability.
