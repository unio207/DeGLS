# Leaf senescence and top leaf death in corn — normal vs disease

Summarized from Purdue University's Corny News Network. Leaves dying late in the season is
often normal maturation, not disease.

## The normal pattern of senescence

Many people assume leaves simply die from the bottom upward. Nielsen notes that leaf
senescence often progresses from **both the bottom and the top** of the plant, with green
leaves remaining in the middle. This simultaneous upper-and-lower pattern is documented in
research and occurs naturally during grain fill.

## The single most useful diagnostic: pattern across plants

This is what separates natural or stress senescence from a leaf disease:

- **Stress-induced senescence** usually affects **all plants** within an affected area of the
  field, uniformly.
- **Disease and insect damage** usually occur **more randomly from plant to plant**, rather
  than affecting every plant in an area.

So before concluding a yellow or dead lower leaf is disease, look at the neighbouring plants.
Uniform across every plant in a patch points to weather, soil or maturity. Scattered and
variable from plant to plant points to a pathogen.

## Causes of abnormal top leaf death

**Drought and heat stress.** Upper leaf death preceded by gray-green coloring and wilting,
when plants cannot maintain leaf health under soil moisture deficits and high transpiration.

**Insect damage.** European corn borer or Southwestern corn borer tunneling, or girdling of
leaf sheaths at stalk nodes.

**Anthracnose.** A fungal infection, identified by black lesions visible on the outer stalk
tissue behind the leaf sheaths.
