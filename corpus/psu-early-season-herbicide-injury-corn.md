# Early season herbicide injury to corn (not a disease)

Summarized from Penn State Extension. Herbicide injury discolors and deforms corn leaves and
is regularly mistaken for disease. It is a chemical injury; no pathogen is present and a
fungicide will do nothing for it.

## Symptoms by herbicide group

**Group 15 (acetamides).** Leaves fail to unfurl properly or stay tightly wrapped; emerged
plants may look "buggy-whipped." Root systems are typically unaffected.

**Group 4 (dicamba).** Soil applications cause twisting, swelling, and onion-leafing of
shoots; roots may be pruned and thickened. Foliar applications cause stunting. Injury is worse
on shallow-planted corn in cool, wet conditions.

**Group 3 (pendimethalin).** Pruned roots, swollen root tips, and stunted plants. Emerged
plants often look purple, from a secondary phosphorus deficiency caused by inhibited root
growth.

**Group 2 (ALS inhibitors).** Stunting with purple to yellow coloring and **interveinal
chlorosis**. Roots show reduced lateral development and look slightly thickened and swollen.

**Group 27 (pigment inhibitors).** **Bleaching** and chlorosis as chlorophyll development is
blocked. Mesotrione applications cause temporary yellowing that typically recovers quickly.

## Distinguishing herbicide injury from other causes

Environmental stress (cool, wet weather), planting depth, soil organic matter, and tank-mix
partners all influence injury severity, and all of these must be considered before attributing
symptoms to a herbicide rather than another cause.

Note that bleached-white or interveinal-chlorotic tissue is not a symptom any of the three
corn foliar diseases in this corpus produce — gray leaf spot, northern corn leaf blight, and
rust all produce discrete lesions or raised pustules, not loss of pigment across whole leaves.
