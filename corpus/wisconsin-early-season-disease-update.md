# Early Season Disease Update and Management Considerations

Badger Crop Connect presentation by Damon Smith, UW-Madison Field Crop Pathologist. (This is a
transcript-derived summary of the corn and decision-tool portions.)

## Corn diseases

### Anthracnose leaf blight

This annual early-season appearance affects corn-on-corn and no-till situations. The disease
targets juvenile leaves in plants younger than V6, representing an abnormal susceptibility window
rather than a predictor of stalk rot risk.

**Management:** Fungicide applications are not economically justified. Wisconsin research showed
the leaf blight phase is not linked to the stalk rot phase. Growers should avoid the treatment
cost and allow plants to outgrow susceptibility as they reach V6-V7.

### Tar spot and general foliar fungicide timing

Early tar spot detection occurred in Kansas and Indiana, earlier than typical historical
patterns. Even under favorable Midwest conditions, early corn growth stages are too immature for
an economically justified fungicide application.

**Scouting strategy:** Prioritize fields that had high tar spot the previous year. Active
scouting determines disease presence and informs later treatment decisions.

**Fungicide application timing:** Early applications fail to protect the grain-producing leaves.
Optimal timing targets VT to R3 growth stages, when the leaves that are making grain need
protection. Because a fungicide is only in the plant about 30 days, early applications require
reapplication and waste money.

**Decision approach:** Combine prediction models with field scouting rather than spraying
preventively early.

## Decision support tools

### Fungicide ROI calculator

The Crop Protection Network's corn fungicide ROI tool uses real-world data and simulations to
give expected net benefits and break-even probabilities. Users can customize yield expectations,
commodity prices, and local fungicide costs.

**Important:** Expected net benefit and break-even probability are distinct metrics that must be
evaluated together. High expected benefit with low break-even probability indicates a high-risk,
high-reward scenario where success is possible but uncertain.

### Digital disease forecast platforms

The standalone Sporecaster, Tarspotter, Sporebuster, and Field Prophet apps have been retired.
Replacements:

1. **Agricultural Forecasting and Advisory System (Wisconsin-specific):** uses Wisconsin's
   WiscoNet weather station network to calculate risk indices from measured weather data. Users
   can toggle between WiscoNet stations and gridded datasets for site-specific predictions.

2. **Crop Protection Network national tool:** operates across corn, soybeans, potatoes, carrots,
   beets, and onions. Features include pin-saving, a 7-day forecast, historical season review,
   and 2.5-kilometer gridded weather data.

Both platforms provide one-stop access to multiple disease prediction models.
