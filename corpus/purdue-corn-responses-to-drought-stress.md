# Corn responses to drought stress (not a disease)

Summarized from Purdue University's Corny News Network. Drought stress discolors and kills
corn leaf tissue without any pathogen involved, and it is one of the common reasons a leaf
looks "sick" in mid-summer.

## Leaf rolling

Leaf rolling is the earliest visible stress indicator. It occurs when leaf transpiration
demand is no longer matched by soil moisture uptake. Rolling reduces water loss but also
reduces the leaf area exposed to sunlight, so photosynthesis drops.

The timing of the rolling tells you how serious it is:

- Rolling that appears in the heat of the day and **recovers** in the evening or under cloud
  cover is **not serious stress**.
- Rolling that persists **from sunrise through dark** indicates **serious stress**.

## Leaf discoloration sequence

Under severe stress the leaf color progression is specific and useful for diagnosis:

**normal green → dark green-gray → gray → straw color (complete death)**

This is a whole-leaf or large-area progression. It is not the discrete, margin-bounded lesion
that a fungal foliar disease produces.

## Timing and yield

Corn response to drought varies with the timing, duration, and severity of the drought. Corn
is **most sensitive to drought within two weeks either side of pollination**. Nielsen's article
tabulates effects across V1-V5, V6-V14, V15-VT, silking, grain fill and post-maturity, covering
direct yield impacts on ears per area, kernels per ear, and kernel weight, plus indirect
effects on photosynthetic capacity and plant uniformity.
