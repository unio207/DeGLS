# Update on corn diseases and fungicide decisions in Iowa

## Diseases observed

Four foliar diseases were observed in Iowa corn: gray leaf spot, northern corn leaf blight, tar
spot, and common rust (with southern rust to the south and west of Iowa). All of these diseases
can be managed in-season with applications of a fungicide.

## Environmental conditions needed for disease establishment

| Disease | Temperature | Moisture requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Common rust | 61-77 °F | > 6 hours leaf wetness |
| Gray leaf spot | 77-88 °F | 11-13 h dew or fog; relative humidity in the canopy > 90% for 12 h |
| Northern corn leaf blight | 65-75 °F | > 6 hours leaf wetness |
| Southern rust | 77-82 °F | > 6 hours leaf wetness |
| Tar spot | 60-70 °F | > 7 hours leaf wetness |

Unpredictable weather patterns — alternating between wet and dry conditions — complicate
forecasting and threshold development.

## Fungicide timing and growth stages

Fungicide application timing presents a real challenge. Most fungicides are applied at or near
tasseling, but the best return on investment on a fungicide application comes when disease is
present in a field on the ear leaf and in the upper canopy of most plants throughout the field
at R5. This timing mismatch means decisions must be made before optimal disease assessment is
possible.

## Decision factors

Consider these factors when deciding whether to apply a fungicide:

- Hybrid susceptibility to disease
- Weather forecasts and field microclimate conditions
- Cropping history and prior disease occurrence in the field
- Financial considerations

Spray if it looks like all three sides of the disease triangle are, or will be, present through
grain fill.

## Predictive tools

Decision-support models include AgPMT.org and the Tarspotter app developed at the University of
Wisconsin, which uses a model to predict the risk of tar spot in a field and the need for a
fungicide application.
