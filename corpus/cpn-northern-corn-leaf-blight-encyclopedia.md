# Northern Corn Leaf Blight of Corn

## Causal organism

Northern corn leaf blight (NCLB) is caused by the fungus *Setosphaeria turcica* (anamorph
*Exserohilum turcicum*). The pathogen exists in at least four races, with race 1 the most
common.

## Symptoms and identification

Symptoms typically appear on the lower leaves first. Lesions are long (1 to 6 inches) and
elliptical. They begin gray-green and become pale gray or tan. Under moist conditions dark
gray spores develop on the lower leaf surface, giving lesions a characteristic "dirty" gray
appearance. Severely affected plants may show complete leaf death, which obscures individual
lesions. Lesions can appear on ear husks, but the kernels themselves are not infected.

Hybrids carrying an *Ht* gene for resistance display smaller, chlorotic lesions that may
develop into linear streaks and rarely produce spores.

## Disease cycle

The fungus overwinters in corn residue. Spores are dispersed by wind and water splash.
Disease progression requires leaf wetness periods longer than 6 hours and moderate
temperatures between 64 and 81 °F.

## Management

**Resistant hybrids.** Two types of resistance exist: monogenic (*Ht* genes, controlled by
single genes) and polygenic (controlled by multiple genes). *Ht*-gene hybrids are susceptible
to certain pathogen races; polygenic resistance protects against all races but is less
absolute.

**Cultural practices.** Crop rotation and reduction of surface residue through tillage
decrease fungal inoculum.

**Chemical control.** Multiple foliar fungicides are labeled for NCLB management.
