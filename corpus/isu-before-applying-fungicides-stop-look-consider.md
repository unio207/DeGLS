# Before applying fungicides to corn: Stop! Look! Consider!

## Stop!

Before applying a fungicide, consider the hybrid's resistance to foliar disease. Common Iowa
foliar diseases include common rust, gray leaf spot, northern leaf blight, and eyespot. Research
from the mid-1990s in southeast Iowa demonstrated that fungicide profitability was strongly
influenced by gray leaf spot susceptibility. Yield benefits and profit potential occurred
primarily on highly susceptible hybrids; intermediate and moderately resistant hybrids showed
insufficient yield gains to justify application costs.

## Look!

Scout fields beginning just before tasseling. Foliar diseases typically initiate on the lower
leaves and progress upward. Focus on the leaves below the ear leaf and the ear leaf itself —
these are the leaves we need to protect, since they contribute 75 to 90 percent of the
carbohydrates to grain fill. Absence of disease on the lower leaves may indicate that a fungicide
application is unwarranted. Continue scouting weekly throughout the season.

## Consider!

### Environmental conditions

Environment significantly influences disease development. Hot, humid weather favors gray leaf
spot. Northern leaf blight infection and spore production occur in wet conditions between
64-81 °F. Dry weather suppresses most fungal disease development.

### Planting date

Earlier infection at earlier growth stages causes greater yield impact. Later-planted corn faces
increased risk for foliar disease losses. In central Iowa, gray leaf spot typically appears on
lower leaves around late July and early August.

### Corn-following-corn fields

Foliar disease pathogens survive winter in crop residue. Infested residue on the soil surface
significantly increases the risk and development of gray leaf spot, northern leaf blight, and
eyespot.

### Fungicide activity and timing

Most fungicides remain effective for 14 to 21 days. Since corn requires approximately 60 days
from silking to physiological maturity, early fungicide applications may lose effectiveness
during critical grain-fill periods.

### Plant health

Research from Illinois showed that fungicides applied in the absence of disease increased yields
by 6.2 bushels per acre — insufficient to cover fungicide and application costs.

### Stalk rots

Foliar fungicides do not directly combat stalk rots, as the causal fungi infect systemically and
available fungicides lack sufficient systemic activity. However, managing foliar disease
indirectly reduces stalk rot prevalence by maintaining photosynthetic tissue and reducing plant
stress.

## Effective fungicide groups

Research demonstrates that fungicides containing strobilurin and/or triazole active ingredients
are most effective against corn foliar pathogens.

## Conclusion

Producers considering fungicide applications for plant health benefits should recognize that a
significant economic response is not assured. Profitability depends on multiple factors.
