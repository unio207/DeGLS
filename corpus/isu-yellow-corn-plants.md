# Yellow Corn Plants — causes that are NOT disease

Summarized from Iowa State University Extension and Outreach Integrated Crop Management.
This article is about yellowing that is *not* caused by a foliar pathogen. General or uniform
yellowing of corn is far more often a nutrient, soil, or weather problem than a disease.

Foliar diseases such as gray leaf spot, northern corn leaf blight, and rust produce discrete
**lesions or pustules** — bounded spots with defined margins on otherwise green tissue. Uniform
chlorosis with no lesion margins is a different kind of problem and the causes below should be
considered first.

## Causes of yellow corn identified by ISU

**Cold temperatures.** Early planting into cool conditions causes a lack of green color in
small plants.

**Wet soils.** Waterlogged conditions restrict root respiration and nutrient uptake. This
produces yellowing along with many other symptoms, including phosphorus deficiency.

**Slow organic matter mineralization.** Cold soil slows the microbial conversion of organic
nitrogen into plant-available forms, causing nitrogen stress and plant yellowing.

**Sulfur deficiency.** Older leaves stay green while the new leaves show yellowing and
interveinal striping. Severe cases yellow the whole plant.

**Continuous corn.** Corn following corn shows more yellowing than corn following soybean,
especially in reduced-tillage systems.

**Potassium deficiency.** Appears on larger plants as yellow to brown coloration on the leaf
margins of older leaves.

**Hybrid selection.** Hybrids differ in their susceptibility to interveinal striping and in
their natural leaf color.

For visual diagnostics the article refers readers to ISU Extension publication IPM 42 on
nutrient deficiencies.
