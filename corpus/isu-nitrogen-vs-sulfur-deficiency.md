# Nitrogen vs sulfur deficiency in corn (not a disease)

Summarized from Iowa State University Extension and Outreach Integrated Crop Management.
Both of these are nutrient problems, not foliar disease. Neither produces lesions.

## Nitrogen deficiency

The classic symptom is yellowing of the **lower, older** leaves, running from the leaf tip
toward the base **down the midrib**, which gives the yellowed area a V shape pointing back
along the leaf. Nitrogen is mobile in the plant, so it is withdrawn from older tissue first
and symptoms start at the bottom of the plant and move up if the deficiency persists.

## Sulfur deficiency

The classic symptom is the opposite position on the plant: yellowing of the **new** leaves in
the whorl, sometimes with interveinal striping, while the lower, older leaves stay uniformly
green.

## Which part of the plant is affected is the key

Nitrogen affects older leaves along the midrib. Sulfur affects newer leaves and may show
striping between the veins. Sawyer notes that in young plants with severe deficiency the two
can overlap and be genuinely hard to tell apart.

## Confirming it in the field

Sawyer's practical test: hand-apply some sulfur fertilizer to one area and nitrogen fertilizer
to another, and see which patch greens up. That directly identifies which nutrient is limiting.

## Contrast with foliar disease

A V-shaped yellowing running down the midrib of a lower leaf, with no bounded lesion and no
pustules, is a nitrogen pattern rather than gray leaf spot, northern corn leaf blight, or rust.
Gray leaf spot lesions are rectangular and vein-bounded; northern corn leaf blight lesions are
cigar-shaped with smooth margins; rust produces raised pustules that rupture the leaf surface.
