# Common Rust of Corn

## Overview

Common rust is a fungal disease caused by *Puccinia sorghi*. It occurs every growing season
and is typically not a problem in hybrid (field) corn.

## Symptoms and identification

Early signs are chlorotic flecks on the leaf surface, which develop into distinctive pustules.
Pustules are powdery and brick-red, oval or elongated, about 1/8 inch long, and may be
scattered sparsely or clustered together. Rust pustules usually first appear in late June.

Leaf tissue around the pustules may become yellow or die, leaving lesions of dead tissue.
These lesions sometimes form a band across the leaf, and entire leaves will die if severely
infected. As pustules mature the red spores turn black, and black pustules continue to break
through the leaf surface. Husks, leaf sheaths, and stalks may also be infected.

## Disease cycle

The fungus survives the winter as spores in subtropical and tropical regions. Spores are
carried long distances by wind and eventually reach the Midwest.

## Conditions that favor disease

High humidity with night temperatures of 65-70 °F and moderate daytime temperatures favor
development. Common rust is usually more severe on seed corn than on commercial hybrid corn.

## Management

- Resistant hybrids and inbreds.
- Foliar fungicides labeled for common rust.
